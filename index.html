How Much Pea Gravel for a Garden Walkway?

A garden walkway is one of the easiest pea gravel projects to plan, because the shape is simple: a long rectangle with a shallow, even depth. The worked example below uses a walkway 30 feet long and 4 feet wide, at 2 inches deep. That is 120 square feet, and the calculator adds a 10% buffer for uneven ground, settling, and corners.

The calculator result

For that walkway, the calculator returns 0.81 cubic yards, or 22.0 cubic feet. At the calculator's density of 2,700 pounds per cubic yard (1.35 tons per cubic yard), that is about 2,200 pounds, or 1.10 tons. Order by cubic yards when the supplier sells by the yard, and check the unit on any quote before you compare prices.

How many bags?

In bags, the same job needs about 44 of the 0.5 cubic foot bags, each about 50 pounds. That is about 0.79 pallets, using the calculator's pallet size of 56 bags. The other sizes on the same result are 55 bags of the 0.4 cubic foot size and 22 bags of the 1.0 cubic foot contractor size.

Bags vs. bulk cost

The calculator's estimated cost for this walkway is $127–$219, bagged versus bulk. The 0.5 cubic foot bags cost $219.12 at $4.98 each, while bulk plus truck delivery costs $127.37, including $85 delivery on bulk at $52 per cubic yard. Bulk saves $92 on this job. The other bag totals are $246.40 for the 0.4 cubic foot bags and $192.50 for the 1.0 cubic foot bags.

When bulk wins

The calculator page puts the bulk breakeven at about 0.39 cubic yards, or about 21 bags. This walkway needs 0.81 cubic yards, well past that point, so bulk is the cheaper choice here. For a much smaller patch, bags can still make sense, because you avoid a delivery fee and can buy only what you need.

Before you order

Measure the length and width again before ordering, and keep the depth even across the whole walkway. A thin spot will show the ground underneath, while a deep spot wastes material. Use edging to hold the round stones in place, and spread the gravel in a thin, even layer rather than dumping it in piles.
